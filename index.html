1. PIC based Microwave Photonic Intelligent Water Quality Monitoring system

Summary: The project proposes a microwave photonic sensing platform for monitoring water quality using silicon photonic integrated circuits. A micro-ring resonator will be employed to detect refractive index changes corresponding to dissolved contaminants such as heavy metals, nitrates, salinity, and industrial pollutants. Microwave photonic interrogation will improve detection resolution while maintaining compact device dimensions. The project will evaluate sensing performance through resonance wavelength shifts, sensitivity, Q-factor, and detection limits using numerical simulations. The layout will be prepared in KLayout for future CMOS-compatible fabrication. The developed sensor has potential applications in environmental monitoring, smart water distribution systems, and industrial wastewater management.

Prerequisites: Silicon photonics, microwave photonics, optical resonators, environmental sensing, Lumerical FDTD, INTERCONNECT, KLayout, MATLAB/Python.

3. Design of Weak Signal Detector Using Quantum Microwave Photonic Technique

Summary: The Design of a Weak Signal Detector Using Quantum Microwave Photonic Technique aims to develop an advanced sensing system capable of detecting extremely weak microwave signals with high sensitivity, precision, and low noise. Conventional electronic signal detection techniques often face limitations due to thermal noise, electromagnetic interference, and restricted bandwidth, making it difficult to detect signals with very low power levels. Quantum microwave photonics combines the principles of quantum mechanics with microwave photonic technology to overcome these limitations. This technology has significant applications in radar systems, satellite communications, wireless communication networks, electronic warfare, radio astronomy, biomedical sensing, and quantum communication, where reliable detection of extremely weak signals is essential.

Prerequisites: Fundamentals of Microwave Photonics and Quantum technology, Python/MATLAB programming, and basic knowledge of signal processing and data analysis.

5. Dynamic Load Balancing Scheme in Multi-UAV-Enabled Wireless Networks

Abstract:

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) equipped with communication modules can act as mobile base stations. This project will focus on load balancing in multi-UAV-enabled wireless networks, where UAVs will serve ground user equipments (UEs) with time division multiple access (TDMA). The UEs will have spatiotemporal heterogeneous requests to transfer data which need to be fulfilled in time, the unfinished part called unfinished user request. To improve the quality of service (QoS) and promote the overall system performance, we will exploit the flexibility of UAVs to propose a flexible and efficient multi-UAV-enabled load balancing (MULB) scheme. Taking the load balancing effect into consideration, the network optimization problem will be formulated as minimizing the maximum unfinished user request by jointly optimizing user association, UAV trajectory, and time slot allocation.

6. Multipath resource allocation strategy in survivable space division multiplexing elastic optical networks

Abstract:

The space division multiplexing elastic optical network (SDM-EONs) can effectively supply the large-capacity for the current optical network. If a link failure occurs, it will bring huge data losses or disruptions for the operator. this project will propose a survivable multipath fragmentation-sensitive fragmentation-aware routing, core and spectrum assignment (SM-FSFA-RSCA) scheme to solving the problem of survivability of the request in SDM-EONs and spectrum fragmentation that caused by the dynamic allocation and release of spectrum resources. We will design a fragmentation-sensitive fragmentation metric (FSFM) to estimate the spectrum fragmentation in the established lightpaths, and design a dynamic routing and core selection strategy for the request to control spectrum fragmentation in the core according to FSFM. We will consider the survivable multipath routing strategy for saving spectrum resources, each path of the multipath can be used as a working path or a protection path. In addition, for spectrum allocation, a survivable multipath with shared spectrum allocation strategy is adopted to maximize the utilization of frequency resources.

7. Optical FBG Sensor-Based System for Low-Flying UAV Detection and Localization

Abstract:

With the recent increase in the threat posed by unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) operating in environments where conventional detection systems such as radar, optical, or acoustic detection are impractical, attention is paid to methods for detecting low-flying UAVs with small radar cross-section (RCS). The most commonly used detection methods are radar detection, which is susceptible to electromagnetic (EM) interference, and optical detection, which is susceptible to weather conditions and line-of-sight. This project will aim to demonstrate the possibility of using passive optical fiber Bragg grating (FBG) as a sensitive element array for low-flying UAV detection and localization. The principle is as follows: an optical signal that propagates through an optical fiber can be modulated due to the FBG reaction on the air pressure caused by a low-flying (even hovering) UAV. As a result, a small target—the drone can be detected and tracked via intensity surge determination.

11. Inverse Design of Photonic Components using Machine Learning Approach

Inverse design reverses the conventional workflow: instead of simulating a given geometry to find its response, it starts from target optical specifications and automatically discovers geometries that achieve them. Machine learning (ML) accelerates this by learning the mapping between device parameters and electromagnetic responses, then inverting it to propose candidates far faster than brute-force sweeps or adjoint-only loops.

Common strategies include discriminative models (direct and tandem neural networks), generative models (VAEs, GANs, diffusion models), and reinforcement learning, often combined with physics-based optimizers like adjoint methods or topology optimization. Hybrid schemes use ML to initialize or regularize the search, while adjoint gradients refine fine features under fabrication constraints such as minimum width and spacing.

Applications span ultra-compact couplers, low-loss bends, broadband splitters, grating couplers, metamaterial absorbers, and even dispersion-engineered structures for nonlinear processes like four-wave mixing in topological photonic crystals. Multi-fidelity ensembles further improve reliability by coupling low-cost surrogate models with high-fidelity simulations or experimental data, enabling robust, foundry-ready photonic components with reduced design cycles.

12. Design and Analysis of Photonic Devices for Optical Neural Networks

Optical neural networks (ONNs) map neural computation onto linear optical transforms and nonlinear activations, aiming for high-throughput, low-latency inference at the speed of light. Photonic devices here must implement programmable unitary/linear transforms (e.g., MZIs, microring meshes), efficient weighting, and low-power nonlinearities or optoelectronic activation stages.

Design focuses on low-loss, phase-stable interferometric meshes with compact footprints, thermal/electro-optic tuning efficiency, and tolerance to fabrication variations. Ring-based weight banks and MZI lattices are analyzed for insertion loss, crosstalk, bandwidth, and power–accuracy trade-offs, often using circuit-level simulators coupled to electromagnetic solvers. For activations, strategies include semiconductor optical amplifiers, two-photon absorption, microheater-based thresholding, or hybrid optoelectronic loops with fast detectors and modulators.

Analysis extends to system-level metrics: MACs per joule, latency, quantization noise, and trainability under realistic component imperfections. Recent work also explores inverse-designed building blocks (e.g., low-crosstalk couplers, compact bends) to shrink mesh area and improve scaling, alongside calibration and control algorithms that mitigate drift and maintain matrix accuracy over time.

13. Design and Analysis of Optical Bio-Sensors

Optical bio-sensors transduce biochemical interactions into measurable optical signals using resonant, interferometric, or evanescent-field platforms. Key designs include microring and photonic crystal cavities, slot waveguides, plasmonic nanostructures, and grating-based sensors, all engineered to maximize sensitivity (nm/RIU or pm/ng·mL⁻¹), figure of merit, and limit of detection while maintaining robustness in fluidic environments.

Design leverages strong field confinement and high Q-factors to enhance light–matter interaction with target analytes. Slot waveguides and photonic crystal defect cavities increase overlap with the sensing region; inverse design further tailors mode profiles and dispersion to boost sensitivity and relax fabrication tolerances. For specificity, surfaces are functionalized with receptors (antibodies, aptamers), and structures are optimized to minimize bulk refractive index drift while amplifying surface-bound signals.

Analysis combines electromagnetic simulations with binding kinetics and noise models to predict dynamic range, response time, and detection limits. Experimental validation includes calibration against known concentrations, assessment of selectivity, and integration with on-chip references and microfluidics for stable, real-time readout suitable for point-of-care and lab-on-chip systems.

14. Design and Simulation of CMOS-Compatible Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (TFLN) E-O  Modulators for Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) Transmitter

The rapid advancement of quantum communication demands compact, high-speed, low-power, and CMOS-compatible electro-optic modulators for realizing scalable and secure Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) systems. Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (TFLN) has emerged as a promising platform owing to its excellent electro-optic properties, low optical loss, and ultra-high modulation bandwidth.

In the proposed project our aim is to design and simulate a CMOS-compatible TFLN Mach–Zehnder electro-optic modulator by optimizing waveguide dimensions and traveling-wave electrodes to achieve high optical confinement, low insertion loss, low half-wave voltage (Vπ), high extinction ratio, and low energy per bit. CMOS-compatible fabrication constraints will also be investigated to facilitate large-scale photonic integration. The optimized modulator will be integrated into a QKD transmitter supporting time-bin and phase encoding, Decoy-State BB84 protocol. The design and performance will be analyzed using Ansys Lumerical MODE/DEVICE, COMSOL Multiphysics, CST Microwave Studio (or Ansys HFSS), and MATLAB/Python.

15. Design and Performance Optimization of a CMOS-Compatible Silicon Electro Absorption Modulator for High-Speed Photonic Integrated Circuits

Electro-absorption modulator (EAMs) is key component of photonic integrated circuits which enables high-speed, low-power optical interconnect and quantum photonic systems. Developing CMOS compatible silicon EAMs facilitates seamless integration with electronic circuits, resulting in compact, scalable, and cost-effective optical transceivers. In this project aims to design and optimize a Si-CMOS-compatible electro-absorption modulator with low insertion loss, high extinction ratio, wide modulation bandwidth, and low driving voltage. The work will involve designing the modulator structure using photonic and semiconductor device simulation tools, followed by optimization of waveguide dimensions, doping profiles, and electrode configurations to enhance modulation efficiency and speed. Optical and electrical characteristics, including insertion loss, extinction ratio, bandwidth, and energy consumption, will be systematically analyzed. The optimized design will be compared with recently reported silicon modulators to assess its suitability for next-generation high speed optical interconnects.

16. CMOS–Photonic Co-Integrated Design of Photodetectors and Transimpedance Amplifiers for Very Low-Photon Optical Detection

Design a CMOS–photonic co-integrated photodetector and transimpedance amplifier (TIA) for detection of low mean photon or weakly coherent photon is a key enabling technology for quantum key distribution (QKD), LiDAR, and ultra-sensitive optical sensors. Co-integrating CMOS electronics with silicon photonic devices enables compact, low-power, high-speed, and scalable receiver front

ends suitable for next-generation photonic integrated circuits.

In this project, our aim is to design and optimize a CMOS–photonic co-integrated photodetector and transimpedance amplifier (TIA) with high sensitivity, very low dark count, wide bandwidth, and low power consumption. The project will begin with the design and simulation of a CMOS-compatible photodetector using photonic/device simulation tools, followed by extraction of a compact model for circuit-level integration. A low-noise CMOS transimpedance amplifier (TIA) will then be designed and co-simulated with the photodetector to optimize gain, bandwidth, noise, sensitivity, and power consumption. Finally, the integrated receiver will be evaluated for weak-photon detection performance under QKD, LiDAR, and ultra-sensitive optical sensing applications.

23. Design and Development of an Electro-Optic Transmitter for Industrial and Environmental Parameters

For the measurement and transmission of industrial parameters such as flow rate, pressure, temperature, etc., an optical transmitting system has a great impact, especially for long-distance transmission through inflammable and hazardous areas. The design of a complete optical system, i.e., both the sensing and transmitting units of optical type, increases the system's cost to a very high level. A combination of a primary electrical-type sensor and a Mach-Zehnder Interferometer (MZI)-based transmitter will be designed. The approach will be capable of measuring parameters such as temperature, humidity, flow rate, or pressure, and transmitting the readings to a remote location via electro-optic modulation in the MZI. The proposed system will be a hybrid type and consists of two sections. The first part is the electrical type, which is responsible for detecting and sensing the measured parameters in the electrical domain (primary), and the second part is responsible for transmitting the measured information in the optical domain (secondary).

24. Project Title: Analysis and Performance Optimization of RF Injection Locked Optoelectronic Oscillator for Stable Microwave Signal Generation

Project Summary: Optoelectronic Oscillators (OEOs) can generate microwave signals with ultra-low phase-noise, which are useful for radar, wireless communication, microwave photonics and sensing systems. However, frequency instability and numerous oscillation modes are commonly observed in conventional fiber-delay OEOs owing to the lengthy cavity delay. The proposed research is to study the performance of an RF injection locked optoelectronic oscillator and to study the influence of injection locking on frequency stability and spectrum purity. The proposed effort is modelling and simulation of fiber delay OEO utilizing Lumerical, Opti-System and MATLAB. We will characterize key performance metrics including the oscillation frequency, Side-Mode Suppression Ratio (SMSR), phase noise, locking range and output RF power under free-running and injection-locked situations. The effect of fiber delay length and injection signal parameters on the performance of the oscillator will be also explored. The project is planned to provide increased frequency stability, reduced phase noise and enhanced SMSR that will help advance high-performance microwave photonic oscillators for next generation communication and sensing applications.

The aim of this project is to develop and study a metal-coated LPFBG sensor for enhanced sensing performance. A thin metallic layer (e.g. gold, silver or other functional metal) will be deposited on the LPFBG surface to enhance the interaction between the guided optical modes and surrounding medium. The student will investigate the influence of parameters of metal coating on shift of resonance wavelength, sensitivity and sensor performance. The project will comprise numerical analysis, sensor fabrication/modification, optical characterisation and experimental evaluation for chemical and refractive index sensing applications. The developed sensor is a compact and sensitive platform for applications in environmental monitoring, chemical detection and biomedical sensing.

The FBG sensing area will be coated with suitable functional materials such as graphene-based nanocomposites, polymers or metal oxide nanomaterials to enhance analyte interaction and sensing sensitivity. The student will investigate the effect of coating parameters on sensor response and evaluate performance characteristics including sensitivity, selectivity, response time and stability. This project will involve sensor fabrication, experimental setups development, analysis of wavelength shift and performance optimisation. Proposed work is to develop an efficient optical fibre sensor for chemical detection, food quality monitoring, environmental analysis and biomedical sensing applications.
